# Scrap Radar Family — Island Worldbuilding Notes

These notes preserve future visual and storytelling ideas for the Island. The goal is to give it more character and history without turning Scrap Radar Family into a fantasy game or distracting from the practical tools.

## Core direction

The Island can borrow subtle visual language from medieval halls, guilds, workshops, old maps, legends, wizardry, dragons, and old-world craftsmanship.

The feeling should be:

**Craft matters. Knowledge is earned by doing. Tools have stories. Builders are remembered. Lessons are passed on.**

Use the theme as atmosphere, symbolism, architecture, and small discoveries rather than as the main product identity.

## Island architecture and atmosphere

Ideas to revisit:

- Timber-framed huts and workshop buildings along the sandy path.
- Heavy wooden doors, beams, stone bases, rope, iron hinges, and weathered signs.
- Iron sign brackets hanging over paths and doorways.
- Carved wooden or brass plaques instead of generic flat web cards where practical.
- Warm lantern-style lighting around halls and paths.
- Old map-room details, parchment-style maps, compass markings, and hand-drawn route lines.
- Workshop and guild styling for places where knowledge and craft are shared.
- Small shields, crests, stamps, or maker marks as recurring visual language.

## Builders Hall

Builders Hall could lean gently toward a guild-hall feeling.

Possible details:

- Timber beams and stonework.
- Contributor plaques that feel engraved or forged rather than simply printed.
- A long gallery of names and contributions.
- Lantern lighting and iron brackets.
- A central crest representing Scrap Radar Family builders.
- The principle **“Credit should follow contribution, not job title.”** displayed like a guild rule or hall inscription.
- **“Every Name Lives On.”** treated as a permanent inscription within the hall.

The hall should remain respectful and grounded. It should celebrate real contributions without fake ranks or exaggerated titles.

## Wall of Names

The Wall of Names can feel like a living memorial wall inside the Island rather than a normal guestbook.

Ideas:

- Approved names illuminate or flash one at a time across stone, timber, brass, or forged-metal styled panels.
- Approved remarks appear with the name when appropriate.
- Names can glow like lantern-lit inscriptions rather than relying only on neon UI.
- A subtle bell, chime, or light sweep could mark a newly approved name.
- Moderation and approval remain underneath the presentation so nothing appears publicly without approval.

## Recovery Labs

Recovery Labs could borrow visual cues from an old workshop or alchemist-style study while keeping all real recovery information modern and scientific.

Possible details:

- Wooden cabinets and labeled drawers.
- Heavy worktables.
- Brass gauges and instrument faces.
- Material samples, diagrams, and reference sheets displayed like a craftsman’s working library.
- Each lab keeps its own identity and folders while sharing the common Island aesthetic.

Science and safety information must remain accurate and clearly separated from decorative fantasy styling.

## Maya’s Office / Control Room

Maya’s office can gradually collect objects that earn their place through the history of the build.

Already-earned recurring objects:

- Coffee on the desk.
- A donut within reach.
- A dive suit hanging by the door, representing the many under-the-hood code dives.
- The established Maya portrait remains the canonical Island portrait.

Future objects should be added because they connect to a real project story, joke, lesson, milestone, or contribution rather than simply filling the room.

Possible old-world touches for the office:

- A project map mounted like an old campaign map.
- A small lantern mixed with modern monitors and control equipment.
- Books, board reference sheets, maps, and recovered material samples arranged like a modern wizard’s working library.

## Dragon motif

A dragon could become a very subtle recurring Island symbol.

Possible uses:

- A tiny dragon worked into an Island crest or seal.
- A carved dragon detail on a sign, doorway, or map border.
- A hidden dragon emblem visitors occasionally notice while exploring.
- A dragon icon reserved for a future major milestone, difficult challenge, or legendary recovery story.

Avoid turning the main Scrap Radar brand into a fantasy-only brand unless a later design decision explicitly calls for it.

## Old-world symbols and wizardry

Use these as flavor rather than literal product mechanics.

Examples:

- Scroll and map styling for lessons or stories in History House.
- Wizard-library atmosphere for deep knowledge and reference areas.
- Workshop, smithy, guild, and map-room references for craft and learning.
- Old-world symbols combined with modern radar, AI, electronics, and recovery technology.

The interesting contrast is **ancient craft + modern intelligence**.

## Design guardrail

The Island should never become so themed that users cannot immediately understand where they are going or what a tool does.

Working rule:

**Fantasy gives the Island soul. Practical tools remain the reason the Island exists.**

Navigation should stay obvious, tappable, mobile-friendly, and fast on iPad and phone. Decorative motion should be gentle and never interfere with accessibility or performance.

## Future review

When the core applications are stable, revisit these notes for a dedicated Island art-direction pass. Consider building one visual prototype first, likely Builders Hall or the sandy-path entrance, before spreading the style throughout the ecosystem.
