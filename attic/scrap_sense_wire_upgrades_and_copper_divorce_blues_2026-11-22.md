# Scrap Radar Family Attic: Scrap Sense and The Copper Divorce Blues
Date: 2026-11-22
Status: Approved concepts, NOT deployed production features.

## Core purpose
Help ordinary scrappers increase *actual net earnings*, not merely quoted price. Scrap Sense teaches grading and upgrades; Board Sense identifies boards; Scrap Radar compares buyer payouts/travel/costs. Keep users' control and honor existing Everything-plan promise. Weigh the extra value against labor and waste weight.

## Jerry's field lessons
- Cut plug/connector ends off insulated scrap wire when buyer grades require it. Check if it raises the price for clean insulated wire.
- Don't throw away valuable ends: some metal terminals and plug blades are yellow brass. Confirm material type (some are plated steel or other metals); save solid brass terminals in a bucket and batch-process them with pliers after fully disconnecting from power.
- A 14/2 NM cable commonly contains two insulated conductors and a bare ground. Removing its outer jacket yields different separate sale categories and lighter residual weight. Example figures offered were approximately $2/lb unsorted Romex, $4/lb certain insulated conductors, $6/lb bare bright, not verified market quotes or guaranteed grades. Calculate actual recovered weights, market quotes, labor and disposal cost. Bare copper must actually qualify for buyer's bare-bright standards.
- For long/thick wire with removable insulation, stripping may pay; for wire with very tightly bonded insulation, even thick wire can be unprofitable. Heat-damaged sections may stick while good sections strip easily: cut off and set aside the tough/damaged portion, safely process the easy remainder. Never burn insulation, process energized cables, or assume oxidized copper is bare bright.
- Core rule: do not spend thirty minutes chasing fifty cents. Choose STRIP NOW / SAVE TO BATCH / SELL INSULATED / PARTIAL UPGRADE.
- Teach common scrap material grades with photo examples, contamination identification, buyer-specific exceptions, and safe upgrade tips; add a 'Don't Throw That Away!' salvage value hint for removed parts.
- Jerry regularly sorts yellow brass, red brass, fixtures, and other metals, while many longtime haulers still bring mixed loads. Sorting can increase payout where buyer grades justify it.

## Buyer Reliability Scorecard
- Compare quoted rate with actual ticket, grades, weights, deductions, waits, travel and vehicle/time costs, then net.
- Treat one questionable downgrade as a question, not a guilt verdict; after repeated unexplained grading surprises warn and show alternatives, but record explanations, user evidence, and legitimate contamination. Never automatically defame a yard or publish an allegation.
- Jerry's example: same brass load type was repeatedly labeled 'dirty' at one buyer on three visits. On third visit, he had it put back on the truck and went to another yard. Many yards instead separate a few contaminated pieces. Buyer-specific rules vary.
- Warning before unloading: previous grading concerns for this material; confirm price and grade, ask about removing contaminated pieces.
- Photo/read a scale ticket with user review and private history; private records by default, optional anonymized aggregates only with consent. Test with small trusted groups before opening wide.

## Entertainment / educational series
Scrap Sense presents 'The Copper Divorce Blues', a completely original playful country-blues song/video starring SPIKE, a cartoon scrapyard worker wearing cowboy hat and overalls, dealing with wire insulation that is 'married' to copper. Core punchline: 'If that insulation has decided it's married to the copper, don't spend half your afternoon arranging the divorce.' Original teaching slogan: 'Know when to strip it, know when to clip it, know when to save it for another day, and know when to quit it.' Refer to the *idea* of judging when to walk away, but do not reproduce or borrow the melody/lyrics of Kenny Rogers's The Gambler. Keep all audio/music original.
